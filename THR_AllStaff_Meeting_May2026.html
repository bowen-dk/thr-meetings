The first of what will be a regular gathering.

National Initiatives

Big things in motion.

Four company-wide efforts that will define what THR looks like in the next five years.

🔬

Comprehensive Addiction Research Network (CARN)

A large federally-funded research grant that would make all THR centers research hubs. Justin has been meeting lawmakers and HHS leadership in D.C. Goal: develop national standards of care for addiction treatment with THR's model as the benchmark.

📱

Treehouse Mobile App

A closed alumni network — think Instagram, exclusively for Treehouse graduates. Post, check in, connect by cohort or branch of service. Already at minimum viable product. Launching to 3,000+ graduates soon, then onboarding every new client at discharge.

💚

Roots to Recovery

Active in Tennessee and North Carolina. Coming to California and Oregon. Mission: fund the financial gap for anyone who can't afford treatment. A national umbrella nonprofit will oversee all state chapters. Goal: tens of millions raised annually so no one is turned away.

📈

Value-Based Care Model

Being developed with Blue Cross Blue Shield NC. THR gets paid based on client outcomes over three years. The longer clients stay in recovery, the more the company earns — with the long-term vision of passing that downstream as staff bonuses tied to client sobriety outcomes.

Staff Feedback

What the team wants to see.

Brandon asked the room directly. Here's what came back — by person, in their words.

Bobby Funk: "Austin Keats — I've never met you. I don't know who you are or where you're at." Robert Mo proposed each meeting include a pod spotlight. Karen Willock suggested rotating spotlights so that over time, every team across all four centers has introduced themselves to the full company.
Karen Willock: "The connection between the pods is essential for us to grow and stay healthy and strong." She proposed joint trainings quarterly or semi-annually across clinical, physical, and literary teams. Brent Botros suggested 15-minute topic presentations each meeting, open for discussion.
Robert Mo: "Our job is so go go go — and it's so hard to take time and reflect." He proposed a recurring wins segment: client sobriety anniversaries, personal milestones, team achievements. Henry Joling added interest in tracking one, two, and three-year sobriety outcomes across all graduates.
Karen Willock: "Burnout and people suffering quietly — in quiet desperation — and we don't know about it until somebody has relapsed or quit." Brandon: "I don't care how strong you are, everyone here needs help. Being able to vocalize those needs — that's what we need to model."
Justin McMillen on behalf of Giovanni Cinfio (Director of HR): Giovanni regularly has to chase people by phone to complete HR tasks. A dedicated HR block each meeting — what's due, what's coming, who to contact — gives visibility without the manual follow-up.
Henry Joling raised a cultural push toward AA as aftercare. Justin's response: "No matter how you feel about AA, if you want people to do what you did, push them toward a place where they can help the next person. That's the core of it — helping other alcoholics and addicts get well."
